Wing 01

Origins — The Lower East Side

The Deadly Art of Survival began as a martial arts system founded by Nathan Ingram in New York City's Lower East Side — and, in DAOS's own words, “quickly evolved into something far greater than a style — it became a cultural movement.”

Exhibit 1.1 — The Founder

Nathan Ingram

Born and raised on the Lower East Side of Manhattan. He began training in 1968 under Angel Crespo Sensei, a traditional Japanese Shotokan instructor. In June 1973 he earned his Shotokan black belt (Shodan); that October he also earned a black belt in Goju Ryu under Timothy Yearwood Sensei.

He went on to become the only African American karate instructor to own a school in the history of Chinatown, NY, and founder and chief instructor of The D.A.S. Karate Federation (1979–present).

Source: Ingram author biography, 25th Edition listing — Amazon. The 1973 founding year is carried consistently across DAOS's own materials.

Exhibit 1.2 — Record & Ranks

On the record

  • 1976–78Rated No. 3 by the World Gung Fu Kickboxing Association; 16-time bare-knuckle champion.
  • Ranks10th Degree Red Belt; 5th Dan Shotokan (ASKD), 3rd Dan Goju Ryu, 5th Dan Jujitsu, 4th Dan Semi Fist–Semi Mantis, senior ranking White Crane, Black Sash Northern 7 Star Praying Mantis.
  • 1979–Founder and chief instructor, The D.A.S. Karate Federation.

Source: Ingram author biography — Amazon, 25th Edition.

Exhibit 1.3 — The Rebirth

2021: Father and son

More than four decades after the founding, 2021 marked the rebirth of The Deadly Art of Survival — spearheaded by Nathan Ingram and his son Jacob Ingram, and reimagined as a global multimedia brand dedicated to preserving martial arts history while celebrating culture, music, entrepreneurship, and the arts.

Today DAOS describes itself as “a martial arts system turned global multimedia brand, home to DAOS Magazine, DAOS TV, radio programming, and The DAOS Foundation.”

Source: DAOS history — deadlyartofsurvival.com

Wing 02

The Film — 1979

Before Wild Style, there was a Super 8 kung fu movie shot on weekends in the Smith Projects — with a rival “disco dojo,” handball courts painted by Lee Quiñones, and Nathan Ingram playing a version of himself.

Exhibit 2.1 — Feature presentation

The Deadly Art of Survival

Nathan Ingram plays a local karate school owner who clashes with drug traffickers posing as the owners of a rival dojo — “Handsome Harry's Disco Dojo,” slogan: Martial Arts With Style. Underneath the plot, the film plays as an intimate snapshot of life on the Lower East Side in 1979, and touches on drugs, gun violence and policing in Black neighborhoods as matter-of-fact parts of the story.

Director Charlie Ahearn was approached by members of the martial arts school to make the film — they were already performing live narrative martial arts scenarios in front of audiences. Most of the budget, Film Forum notes, went to “buying pizza for the kids.”

Exhibit 2.2 — The handball courts

Lee Quiñones & Howard the Duck

While filming, Ahearn came across Lee Quiñones' freshly painted handball-court murals — including a Howard the Duck mural — and included them in the film. Quiñones arrived on his motorbike during filming, introduced himself, and Ahearn invited him to participate.

Source: Hyperallergic, “The Story of Hip-Hop's Film Birth” · Film Forum

Exhibit 2.3 — The road to Wild Style

One film led to the other

1979 — DAOS film→ 1980 — Times Square Show posters→ Fab 5 Freddy meets Ahearn→ 1983 — Wild Style

Posters for The Deadly Art of Survival at the 1980 Times Square Show caught Fab 5 Freddy's eye; that meeting led to the plan for Wild Style — the first hip-hop feature film — in which Quiñones starred as Zoro and Nathan Ingram appeared as a cop in the yard. Ahearn has said this film “led directly to the making of” Wild Style.

Source: Hyperallergic · Cinema Paradiso

Exhibit 5.3 — The Foundation

The DAOS Foundation

The nonprofit behind DAOS, organized for charitable and educational purposes. Its mission: the preservation, documentation, and advancement of cultural history — including contemporary cultural movements, art, music, design, film and social expression — through exhibitions, public events, research, radio, TV and film, education, and the archiving of artifacts and oral histories.

Source: DAOS Foundation mission statement, provided by Jacob Ingram, Nov 4, 2026.

Exhibit 5.4 — The next building

A museum you can walk into

The Foundation's mission gives it the long-term goal of establishing a museum or cultural center dedicated to preserving and presenting cultural history for public education and community enrichment.

No site, timeline or budget has been announced — this digital museum is the first room of that building, and it is open today.

Wing 06

The Convention — DAOS CON

What started as a gathering on May 14, 2022 at Mariner's Temple Baptist Church in Chinatown slowly became DAOS CON — the convention where martial arts meets comics, gaming, music and awards. Created by Nathan and Jacob Ingram. From the third convention on, home is 551 Grand Street on the Lower East Side.

Mariner's Temple Baptist Church, Chinatown

The first DAOS CON. Demos, seminars and reunions — “a lot of these guys hadn't seen each other in 10 plus years.” Chasity Ingram and Evelyn Bermudez ran the food.

About this museum

The Daos Museum is a digital museum of the Deadly Art of Survival: the system founded by Nathan Ingram in 1973, the 1979 Charlie Ahearn film, DAOS Magazine, and the people and music around them. Every exhibit is drawn from a published source, linked on its plaque. Where sources disagree — the film's runtime, its shooting year — the museum shows the disagreement instead of picking a winner.
